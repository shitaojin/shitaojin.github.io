This theme extends architectural programming from individual buildings to community-scale social infrastructure. The
central question is how children, residents, and everyday users can be represented in urban renewal decisions, especially
when their needs are distributed across streets, neighborhood centers, public spaces, safety conditions, and emotional
experience.

## Research Content

The value-based programming work identifies how community environmental features shape children's perceived well-being.
It combines street-view imagery, semantic recognition, and multi-stakeholder questionnaires to connect spatial features
with environmental, safety, psychological, participation, and accessibility dimensions. The result is a framework that
can guide planning and design teams from abstract well-being goals to concrete renewal priorities.

The neighborhood-center research uses online public reviews to understand how residents perceive renewal. Sentiment and
topic modeling reveal how community services, living amenities, environmental quality, aging facilities, commercial
homogenization, and operation models influence public experience. This work is important because neighborhood centers
are everyday social infrastructure, and their performance is increasingly visible through accumulated digital traces.

The child-friendly street research develops deep learning-based evaluation systems. It links abstract perception
features with concrete streetscape characteristics, using Shanghai streets as the empirical context. This approach shows
how AI-based urban analytics can support child-friendly street diagnosis and improvement.

## Outcomes

This theme provides a scalable research framework for child-friendly communities and urban renewal. It demonstrates how
architectural programming can incorporate public perception data, AI image analysis, and value-based design logic to
serve inclusive planning.
