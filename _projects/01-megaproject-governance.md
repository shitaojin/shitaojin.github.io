This theme studies mega construction projects as socio-technical systems. Such projects are not governed only by contracts
and engineering schedules; they are also shaped by public concerns, stakeholder power relations, risk perception,
uncertain environments, and evolving social legitimacy. My research uses computational methods to turn these complex and
often fragmented signals into decision evidence.

## Research Content

The public-concern strand develops an integrated topic and sentiment modeling approach. It tracks how public attention
changes over time, compares sentiment models, and couples topic importance with emotional fluctuation. This is designed
to help project organizations identify which issues require communication, mitigation, or redesign at different project
stages.

The stakeholder-governance strand constructs stakeholder networks and evaluates actor importance using structural
metrics and multi-criteria decision-making. This work recognizes that megaproject stakeholders do not influence outcomes
only through formal authority; their position in networks, access to information, and capacity to coordinate others all
shape governance performance.

The risk and complexity strand develops quantitative models for uncertainty. Risk identification is linked with gray
fuzzy group decision-making and Monte Carlo simulation, while project complexity is measured through technical,
organizational, goal, environmental, cultural, and information dimensions. These studies collectively support earlier
warning, better prioritization, and clearer governance interventions.

## Outcomes

Together, this theme establishes a computational governance agenda for megaprojects. It links public sentiment,
stakeholder coordination, risk assessment, and complexity measurement into a coherent evidence base for infrastructure
management and policy-oriented research.
