education. It grew from my master's research and continues to inform my broader interest in evidence-based design
evaluation and human-centered architectural programming.

## Research Content

The classroom studies compare traditional lecture classrooms and active learning classrooms, asking how visibility,
layout, furniture, technology, comfort, and interaction affordances shape students' satisfaction and engagement. These
papers provide quantitative evidence that learning-space design affects not only comfort but also perceived learning
performance and willingness to participate.

The informal learning perspective broadens active learning beyond scheduled class time. It examines how students use
active learning classrooms and adjacent spaces as full-time learning environments, connecting flexibility, accessibility,
social interaction, and sustainable campus learning behavior.

The academic library and campus environment studies extend the scope to campus-scale learning infrastructure. They
analyze how library spatial attributes, service environments, and innovation-oriented campus planning support student
experience and talent cultivation.

## Outcomes

This theme strengthens my methodological foundation in user-centered evaluation, survey-based evidence, and built
environment performance assessment. It also provides a basis for connecting learning environment evaluation with
architectural programming and AI-supported design decision-making.
